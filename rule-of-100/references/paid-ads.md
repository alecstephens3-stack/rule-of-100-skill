# Paid ads: Hormozi's method

A summary of the paid ads chapter of *$100M Leads*, from the transcript linked in `the-rule.md`. The daily 100 here is 100 minutes spent making ads. Ads come last in the book's order, after warm reach-outs, content and cold reach-outs, because they cost money the other three can earn first.

## His steps

1. Pick a platform you already use, where your customers can be targeted, and that you can afford.
2. Target people who look like your existing customers, then narrow with filters.
3. Make the ad: a call-out, then value, then a call to action.
4. Send them to a simple page that matches the ad and collects their contact details.

## The parts of an ad

- **Call-out.** Get the right person to notice. In words: naming who it's for, a question they'd answer yes to, an if-then statement, or a striking result. Without words: contrast, people who look like the customer, the setting.
- **Value.** Why it is worth their attention: what they get, from whose point of view, and when.
- **Call to action.** Tell them exactly what to do next, and make it quick and easy.

He says that putting his effort into the first five seconds made his own ads far more efficient, so most of the making time goes into call-outs: many openings on a small number of main ads.

## His numbers

- Spend $100 on the first ad to learn how the platform works.
- Study about 50 ads from the platforms' public ad libraries before making your own.
- He says businesses tend to struggle when a customer is worth less than about three times what it costs to get them. He presents that as a pattern he has observed, not a rule.
- He names three phases: track money, lose money, print money. Losing money at the start is expected.

Budget rules (how much to test with, when to stop an ad) are in the chapter itself. This summary does not restate them, because the transcript garbles several of the terms. Read the book for those.

## Mistakes he names

- Treating a sales problem as an ad problem.
- Blaming ads when the offer or the business model is the problem.
- Quitting after early losses.

## What this means for the skill

- The count is minutes spent making ads: writing call-outs, recording, editing, building the page. Watching the dashboard doesn't count.
- The skill can draft call-outs and variations in bulk for the person to record or approve, and keep the list of what has been tested.
- Money is the user's decision every time. The skill never raises a budget, starts a campaign or adds a payment method without a yes that names the amount.
- For the weekly review, log leads and spend as reported by the ad platform, and say which call-outs were tested and which stay.
