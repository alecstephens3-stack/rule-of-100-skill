# Warm reach-outs: Hormozi's method

A summary of the warm outreach chapter of *$100M Leads*, from the transcript linked in `the-rule.md`. Warm means people who already know you. He says this is where anyone new should start, and that the goal at this stage is to learn, not to earn.

## His steps, in order

1. Get your list: every contact in your phone, every email account, every social following.
2. Pick the platform where you have the most contacts.
3. Personalize the greeting with something real about the person.
4. Reach out to 100 people every day.
5. When they reply, warm them up: acknowledge what they said, compliment them on it, ask another question (he calls this ACA). The questions lead toward what you offer.
6. Make the offer by asking whether they know anyone who has the problem you solve. You are not asking them to buy. Some will say "me".
7. Make it the easiest offer in the world: free, for a small number of people.
8. When one platform's list is done, start again on the next-biggest platform.
9. Start charging.
10. Keep the list warm by staying in touch.

## His numbers

- 100 reach-outs a day.
- Follow up with people who don't reply up to three times, once a day for three days, or until they respond.
- Make the offer after three or four exchanges.
- The first five customers are free. Then 80% off for the next five, 60% off for the five after, 40% off for the five after that.
- 1,000 contacts is ten days of first messages.

## His benchmark

100 warm reach-outs get about 20 replies. About one in five of those take the free offer, so four people. About one of those becomes a paying customer later.

Use this in the weekly review to find the weak step. Far fewer than 20 replies per 100 points at the greeting or the list. Plenty of replies and nobody taking the offer points at the offer or at asking too early.

## Mistakes he names

- Trying to look bigger or more advanced than you are.
- Putting off the first hour of reach-outs.
- Not asking why when someone says no.

## What this means for the skill

- The action that counts is a first message to one person. His follow-ups don't count toward the 100 unless the user decides they do. Either way, queue them: a person who got a first message on Monday gets a follow-up on Tuesday if they haven't replied. Record them with `done --follow-up`.
- A greeting needs one real thing about the person. The skill can only see what the user gives it, so ask for the list with whatever notes they have (how they know each person, the last thing they talked about). Where there is nothing real to use, write the rest of the message and leave one clearly marked gap for the user to fill. Never make up a detail about someone.
- Replies need a human answer the same day. The skill drafts the acknowledge, compliment, ask reply and the user sends it.
- Warn at setup when the list will run out, and line up the next platform's list before it does.
